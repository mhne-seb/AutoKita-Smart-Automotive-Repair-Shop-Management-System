# Service Procedures — AutoKita Knowledge Base

## Oil Change
Service Type: Preventative Maintenance
Recommended Interval: Every 5,000 km (conventional oil) or 10,000 km (synthetic oil).

Steps:
1. Warm up the engine for 2 minutes to thin the oil.
2. Drain old oil from the drain plug (usually 14mm or 17mm bolt under the engine).
3. Remove and discard the old oil filter.
4. Install new oil filter (hand-tight plus 3/4 turn).
5. Reinstall drain plug with new washer. Torque to spec (typically 25–30 Nm).
6. Fill with correct oil grade and quantity (check owner manual — typically 3.5L to 5L).
7. Start engine, check for leaks, verify oil pressure light goes off.
8. Check oil level with dipstick after 1 minute.

Common Oil Grades in Philippines:
- 5W-30: Most modern petrol engines
- 5W-40: Turbocharged engines or heavy use
- 10W-40: Older engines or high-mileage vehicles
- 15W-40: Diesel engines, commercial vehicles

Philippines Note: Change oil more frequently if driving mostly in heavy traffic (stop-and-go) — every 3,000–4,000 km recommended.

## Brake Pad Replacement
Service Type: Safety-critical repair
Replacement indicator: When pad thickness is below 3mm or when brake wear indicator squeals.

Steps:
1. Loosen lug nuts before lifting vehicle.
2. Lift and secure the vehicle on jack stands.
3. Remove wheel.
4. Remove caliper bolts (usually 2 bolts — 12mm or 14mm).
5. Slide caliper off the rotor. Do NOT let it hang by the brake hose — use a hook or rest on a surface.
6. Remove old brake pads from the caliper bracket.
7. Compress caliper piston using a C-clamp or brake piston tool (open bleeder screw if needed to avoid forcing fluid back).
8. Install new pads with anti-squeal shim if provided.
9. Reinstall caliper. Torque caliper bolts to spec (typically 25–35 Nm for sliding pin bolts).
10. Pump brake pedal until firm before moving the vehicle.
11. Bed-in new pads: 6–8 moderate stops from 50 km/h to 10 km/h. Allow brakes to cool between cycles.

## Brake Rotor Inspection & Replacement
Replacement criteria: Below minimum thickness (stamped on rotor edge), deep grooves, cracks, or severe heat spotting.
Typical minimum thickness: 22mm for front, 10mm for rear (varies by vehicle).

Steps:
1. Follow brake pad steps 1–5.
2. Remove the caliper bracket bolts (larger bolts — typically 17mm or 19mm).
3. Slide rotor off the hub (may require light hammer taps if stuck with corrosion).
4. Clean hub face with a wire brush.
5. Install new rotor. Apply anti-seize to hub contact surface only — never on braking surfaces.
6. Reinstall bracket, caliper, pads, and wheel.

## Transmission Fluid Change
Service Type: Preventative Maintenance
Recommended Interval: Every 40,000–60,000 km (automatic), every 30,000 km (manual).

Automatic Transmission (ATF):
1. Drain ATF via drain plug or by dropping the pan.
2. Clean the pan and replace the transmission filter if accessible.
3. Reinstall pan with new gasket.
4. Refill via dipstick tube (if equipped) or fill plug to spec.
5. Check ATF level with engine warm and in Park position.

Manual Transmission:
1. Remove drain and fill plugs (both on gearbox body).
2. Drain old gear oil.
3. Reinstall drain plug.
4. Pump new gear oil (typically 75W-90 GL-4 or GL-5) into fill hole until it overflows.
5. Reinstall fill plug.

Philippines Note: In hot climate and heavy traffic conditions, ATF breaks down faster. Consider changes at 30,000 km intervals.

## A/C System Recharge
Service Type: Maintenance / Repair
Tools Required: A/C manifold gauge set, refrigerant (R-134a for most vehicles pre-2020, R-1234yf for newer)

Steps:
1. Connect low-side gauge (blue hose) to the low-pressure port (suction side, larger line).
2. Check system pressures with engine off.
3. Start engine and set A/C to maximum cold.
4. Read low-side and high-side pressures.
   - Correct low side: 25–40 PSI
   - Correct high side: 150–250 PSI
5. If low, connect refrigerant can to manifold and add refrigerant slowly until pressures are within spec.
6. Check for leaks at all connections and the compressor shaft seal.

Philippines Note: A/C compressors fail more frequently in tropical climates due to continuous heavy use. Inspect compressor clutch and belt annually.

## Battery Testing & Replacement
Service Type: Inspection / Replacement
Battery Life: 2–3 years average in Philippine heat (vs. 4–5 years in temperate climates).

Testing:
1. Measure voltage across terminals with engine off: 12.4–12.7V = healthy, below 12.0V = discharged/weak.
2. Load test with a battery tester: should hold above 9.6V under load.
3. Check terminals for corrosion (white/greenish buildup).

Replacement:
1. Disconnect negative (black) terminal first.
2. Disconnect positive (red) terminal.
3. Remove hold-down bracket.
4. Lift out old battery (heavy — use proper lifting technique).
5. Clean battery tray and terminal clamps.
6. Install new battery.
7. Connect positive first, then negative.
8. Apply terminal protector spray or petroleum jelly to terminals.

Philippines Note: Tropical heat is the primary battery killer. Test battery annually after 2 years.

## Wheel Alignment
Service Type: Maintenance
Recommended: After hitting large potholes, after replacing steering/suspension components, every 20,000 km.

Parameters:
- Camber: Vertical tilt of wheel (negative camber tilts inward at top).
- Toe: Direction wheels point relative to vehicle center (toe-in = front edges closer).
- Caster: Angle of steering axis (affects straight-line stability).

Symptoms of misalignment:
- Vehicle pulling to one side
- Uneven tire wear
- Steering wheel off-center when driving straight

Note: Alignment requires a laser or computerized alignment rack. Not a DIY procedure.

## Timing Belt / Chain Inspection & Replacement

### Timing Belt (rubber, replacement required)
Replacement Interval: Every 60,000–100,000 km (check vehicle-specific manual).
Philippine recommendation: 60,000 km due to heat accelerating belt degradation.

Warning: Interference engines can suffer catastrophic damage if the belt breaks. Always replace at or before the recommended interval.
Water pump is typically replaced at the same time (labor already done — cost-effective).

### Timing Chain (metal, lasts longer)
Symptoms of wear: Rattling on cold start (chain slap), P0340 or P0335 codes.
Repair: Replace chain, tensioner, and guides as a set.

## Suspension Inspection
Components to inspect every 20,000 km:
- Shock absorbers / struts: Check for oil leakage, bounce test (car should settle after 1–2 bounces).
- Ball joints: Check for play (jack up wheel, grab top and bottom, push/pull — should have no play).
- Tie rod ends: Grab wheel at 9 and 3 o'clock positions, push/pull — should have no play.
- CV axle boots: Check for cracks or grease flung out. Replace boot or axle if cracked.
- Stabilizer bar links: Check for loose or broken links.

Philippines Note: Philippine roads (potholes, speed bumps, flood conditions) accelerate suspension wear significantly vs. highway driving.
